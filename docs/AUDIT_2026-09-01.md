# Audit — state of the project, 2026-09-01

Covers Phases 0 and 1 as built, at commit `a482e7b`. Every number here was
measured during the audit, not recalled: the commands are reproducible from the
repository and named where it matters. Where something could not be verified, it
says so rather than being omitted.

A rendered version of this audit is published at
<https://claude.ai/code/artifact/465890cd-8f0c-456f-86bc-efbf92e22478> — this
markdown file is canonical, because it is versioned alongside the decision log it
cites.

**Verdict in one line.** The household calculator is independently validated and
publishable. The population is structurally sound, reconciles on income tax, and
is not fit to publish levels for three of its benefit lines — for a cause that is
measured, documented, and not fixable by anything short of better microdata.

## 5. Defects found and fixed

Every one was found by measuring against reality, not by reading code. Magnitudes
are as measured at the time.

| | Defect | Mechanism | Magnitude |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-007 | Collapsing surplus barème brackets onto the top one | `ParameterScale` → `MarginalRateTaxScale` **sums** the rates of brackets sharing a threshold. Three 40 % brackets became one at 120 % | A 3-bracket reform taxed a 240 k€ household **158 160 € instead of 58 720 €**, silently, no error anywhere |
| D-006 | `caseT` (parent isolé) never set | Worth half a fiscal part; `nbptr` multiplies the `isol` parameter by it and infers nothing | A lone parent at 40 k€ with one child was shown **1 073 € of tax instead of 154 €** |
| D-005 | Benefits assessed on empty reference periods | RSA/PPA look back a quarter, aide au logement twelve months; the spec's wiring filled only the simulated year | Disposable income **+11 %** for a 30 k€ renter; phantom January RSA for a 240 k€ earner |
| — | Pensions and unemployment computed then dropped | `to_openfisca.py` wired only `salaire_de_base`; every guard still passed | 15.96 M pensioners appeared to live on nothing → RSA **3× published** |
| — | Household weight taken as the **sum** of member weights | Should be the mean; EXTRIAN is individual but constant within a dwelling | Implied a population of **120 M**; every euro aggregate **×2.2** |
| — | PPS sampling rescaled on top of self-weighting | PPS is already self-weighting; rescaling double-counts heavy households | All aggregates **×2.8** |
| — | Two different notions of "child" conflated | INSEE's "enfant d'une famille" counts any age; tax rattachement stops earlier | Over-generated under-15s by **20 %** — one invented child per employed 21-24-year-old at home |
| D-017 | Raking ordered after incomes | Reweighting moved the wage bill off the target incomes had just been scaled onto | A spent calibration margin that no longer held |
| D-017 | Household typology mapped every `autre` to *complexe* | Adult children counted as unrelated persons | **2.6 M** households vs a census **1.1 M**; worst margin miss 133 % → 41 % |
| D-017 | `HOUSEHOLDS_BY_TYPE` did not sum to its own total | Off by 200 after rounding | Makes IPF chase a contradiction |
| D-017 | Ménage count shown as **validation** while raking targets it | And against the 2022 figure, not the 2023 one raking uses | Flattered the residual to +0.0 %; true value **−0.8 %** |
| this audit | Report rounded ratios to integers | `_fmt` treats ratios and euro amounts alike under the `raw` unit | The interdecile ratio printed as **"5" against a published "4"** when the values are 4.88 and 3.50 — on the page whose job is stating how far off the model is |

Two patterns run through this list and are worth naming.

**Ten of these twelve produced no error of any kind.** Plausible numbers, all
guards green. That is the failure mode this project is most exposed to, and the
reason the reconciliation report exists at all.

**The adversarial audit of the statistical sources earned its keep.** All six
external-data agents came back `SCOPE_PROBLEM`, including a superseded
national-accounts vintage for the wage-bill *calibration target* and an RFR figure
that was per-part rather than total.

## 6. Known defects **not** fixed

| Defect | Cause | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| RSA +106 % | **13.5 M adults have no modelled income**: 3.87 M self-employed, 9.6 M inactive below the retirement proxy | Zero-income adults form zero-income familles and claim RSA |
| Interdecile D9/D1 +39 % | The same 13.5 M sit far below their real counterparts | The decile table is not a description of French inequality |
| Foyers fiscaux −15.7 % | Tax units inferred from dwelling structure; 0.3 M non-resident foyers not modelled at all | Under-counts filing units |
| AF −25 % | AF-eligible family count is close (−11 %); AF is income-modulated and the wage calibration scaled wages up 9 % | Spend gap larger than the count gap |
| No self-employment income | Out of Phase 1's v1 income scope | 3.87 M people at zero |
| No capital or property income | Explicitly out of scope (§4.2) | Top of the distribution is a construction |
| No réductions or crédits d'impôt | Not modelled | 19.5 bn € nationally; why IR is compared to the barème |
| Rent is an assumption | Anchored on a published €/m² and an **invented surface rule** | Weakest input in the housing-benefit chain |
| Age is banded | EEC gives five groups; exact ages drawn inside them | Thresholds not on a band edge are fuzzy per household |
| CAF cross-check not done | No equivalent to the DGFiP simulator was run | Benefit levels have no independent check |

All are recorded in D-015 and stated in the UI's L1 banner and in the
reconciliation report. **The population reports reform deltas, never baseline
levels of RSA, PPA or the income distribution.**

## 7. Deviations from specification

| Spec says | Built | Why |
|---|---|---|
| §3.4 collapse surplus brackets onto the top rate | Zero-rate them, plus a read-back check (`verify_bareme`) | The prescribed recipe produced a 120 % top rate (D-007) |
| §3.2 no mention of `caseT` | Tri-state `parent_isole`, inferred from household shape | Lone parents were over-taxed by 919 € (D-006) |
| §0.5 `historique_revenus` off by default | On by default; three goldens re-anchored | Single-year wiring is not defensible for anyone, and the population cannot use it at all (D-011) |
| §4.4 one foyer + one famille per ménage | One ménage per dwelling-quarter, one tax unit per **adult unit** | 18 226 households (9 %) contain an adult who is neither partner nor rattachable child; folding them in inflates the QF and zeroes real liability (D-013) |
| §4.3 raking, three margin sets | Two margin sets; the wage-decile set replaced by income-level scaling | D-017's ruling; the third dies with ERFS |
| §5 report as internal table | Report **plus** the same table rendered in the UI | The credibility page belongs next to the number |

Every deviation is logged with its measurement. None was made silently.

## 8. Calibration ledger — what has been spent

The rule that makes the reconciliation table mean anything: a margin used to
calibrate can never afterwards be evidence.

**Spent (4):** total gross wages 1 111.65 bn € · declared pensions 400.4 bn € ·
unemployment benefit 35.9 bn € · households by type (and hence the household
count) 31.27 M · children under 15 and children per family.

**Not spent, and therefore able to say something:** the income-tax yield, foyer
counts, every benefit total and caseload, the niveau-de-vie distribution and the
interdecile ratio.

**The discipline held once under pressure and it is worth recording.**
`RETIREMENT_AGE` moves simulated income tax from +14.0 % (at 60) to +10.5 % (at
55). It was chosen on the count criterion — which cut best reproduces DREES's
~17 M pensioners — and `incomes.py` records the reasoning in that order. Choosing
it the other way would have converted a validation line into a calibration target
through the back door. This is easier to fall into than it looks.

## 9. Coverage and gaps

**Three modules have no tests at all — 753 lines.**

| Module | Lines | Risk |
|---|---:|---|
| `population/reconcile.py` | 345 | Generates the credibility page. A formatting or tagging bug here misreports the model's own honesty — and one already occurred (the Ménages mis-tagging, §5) |
| `population/cli.py` | 265 | The only interface to build and reconcile. Exercised by hand, never asserted |
| `population/ingest.py` | 143 | The manifest gate. Verified by hand once (a modified file is refused); no standing test |

Everything else is exercised. `reconcile.py` is the one that should be fixed
first: it is the module whose failure mode is *misstating how wrong the model is*.

**CI has never run.** There is no git remote, so `.github/workflows/test.yml` has
never executed. Simulated locally during this audit by removing `data/` entirely:
**130 passed, 11 skipped** — the fixture carries the population tests and the
API's population tests skip cleanly. The workflow is plausible but unproven on a
real runner, in particular the Python 3.11 install with `setuptools >= 77`.

## 11. What could not be verified

Stated so nobody mistakes silence for assurance.

* **CI on a real runner** — no remote exists.
* **The reconciliation at full N since raking.** The committed comparison ran on a
  50 000-household sample; the full-population run was OOM-killed. Sampling noise
  is about a point, which is larger than most of the raking shifts.
* **Benefit levels against any independent source.** The DGFiP simulator covers
  income tax only; the CAF equivalent has not been run.
* **Anything about firms.** `verify_firm_engine.py` passes, but no Phase 2 code
  has been written or reviewed here.
* **Behaviour of the whole under a second reviewer.** One review exists
  (`REVIEW_2026-08-29.md`); its rulings are implemented, and its numbering
  collision with D-017 was resolved in its favour.

## 12. Recommended next actions

1. **Test `reconcile.py`.** The module that reports how wrong the model is, is the
   one with no tests, and it has now carried **two** real defects — the Ménages
   mis-tagging and the ratio rounding, the second found only because this audit
   went to check a number against its source.
2. **Re-run `reconcile --compare-unraked` at full N** on a machine with headroom,
   and replace the sampled comparison column.
3. **Send the ERFS request.** The plan is written (`PHASE1C_ERFS.md`); the
   compliance guard is in place; the waiting window is the natural slot for the
   remaining Branch-C work. It is the only thing that fixes §6's top three rows.
4. **Run the CAF cross-check** the way the DGFiP one was run. Benefit levels
   currently have no independent validation at all.
5. **Push to a remote** so CI runs at least once before it is trusted.

Phase 2 material (spec, handoff, firm gate) is in the tree and the firm gate
passes, but no Phase 2 code exists. That is a separate piece of work and is not
audited here.
